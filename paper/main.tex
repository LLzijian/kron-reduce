\title{Energy-Structured Grey-Box Dynamic Equivalencing and Operator Selection for Post-Fault Power Systems from 100-ms Observations}
% Author names, affiliations, and funding require author input.
\author{Author information pending}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Forecasting eight seconds of post-fault rotor motion from 100~ms of observations
requires resolving the first swing while maintaining a numerically reliable
free rollout.
We propose an energy-structured reduced-order model (ES-ROM) that couples a
shifted-sine network potential in center-of-inertia coordinates with
positive-semidefinite damping and reciprocal machine-local memory, yielding an
explicit ordinary differential equation with the supply bound
\(\dot H\le v^\top p\).
A smooth-radius causal Gauss--Newton initializer, integral momentum screening,
gradient-balanced backbone fitting, and closed-loop pruning select
machine-local angle-dependent damping from a five-operator library.
On 94 held-out IEEE 39-bus events, ES-ROM confirms 29 of 32
instabilities (90.62\%) with zero false alarms among 62 stable events,
94 numerically qualified forecasts, and 96.81\% overall accuracy, leading the
compared zero-false-alarm trajectory models, including Graph-GRU (26/32),
and improving recall by 12.50 percentage points over the hard-radius
baseline (95\% fault-group bootstrap interval \([2.78,27.27]\)).
The same frozen equations yield a median dominant-mode frequency error of
0.0318~Hz, reproduce 27 of 29 jointly triggered loss-of-synchronism cuts
exactly, and give a forecast-origin mechanical-energy area under the receiver
operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.9526.
\end{abstract}

Dynamic equivalencing, energy-based modeling, equation discovery,
transient stability, power system dynamics.

\section{Problem Formulation and Causal Forecast Protocol}
\label{sec:problem}
Consider the electromechanical motion of ten synchronous generators after a
fault has cleared and the network topology has been restored. Write
\(M=\diag(m_1,\ldots,m_{10})\), \(m=M\mathbf{1}\), and
\(P=I-m\mathbf{1}^\top/(\mathbf{1}^\top m)\). With unwrapped rotor angles
\(\delta\) and a reference equilibrium \(\delta^\star\), the relative state is
\(q=P^\top(\delta-\delta^\star)\), \(v=P^\top\dot\delta\). Then
\(m^\top q=m^\top v=0\). The transpose pair \(P^\top,P\) acts on states and
generalized forces, respectively, and \(v^\top P=v^\top\) on the COI subspace.
Electrical and control dynamics omitted from the retained rotor equations
enter through the effective couplings, damping, and hidden states below.

Set \(t=0\) at fault clearing and \(t_o=0.10\)~s at the forecast origin. The
predictor receives 11 frames of \((q,v)\) sampled every 10~ms through
\(t_o\), the equilibrium, the inertia, graph priors, and the vector \(p\) of
mechanical-power deviations available at \(t_o\). The same value \(p\) is held
constant during history reconstruction and forecasting. After inferring the
hidden state from the 11 frames, the predictor sets the visible state to the
last measured frame and integrates the shared frozen ODE freely for 8~s.
Electrical-power measurements and future mechanical-power schedules are
outside the predictor input. The future trajectories and their available
observation lengths enter only offline objectives and scoring. Fault
identities specify groups, and stability labels are used for weighting
training terms and evaluating forecasts.

For event \(e\) and a window of \(n_e\) samples, the normalized
root-mean-square error (NRMSE) is
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_e=
 \left(\frac{1}{20 n_e}\sum_{j=1}^{n_e}
 \left[\frac{\|\hat q_j-q_j\|_2^2}{s_q^2}
     +\frac{\|\hat v_j-v_j\|_2^2}{s_v^2}\right]\right)^{1/2},
 \label{eq:nrmse}
\end{equation}
with \(s_q=0.476753\)~rad and \(s_v=1.003367\)~rad/s.
We average \(\epsilon_e\) with equal event weights over the stated cohort.
Stable events use a fixed horizon. Unstable events use the observed
post-origin interval up to 8~s.
On the concatenated history and forecast, loss of synchronism requires a span
of \(q+\delta^\star\) above \(\pi\) and a maximum absolute COI speed
\(\max_i |v_i|/(2\pi\cdot60)\) above \(0.02\)~p.u.\ for five consecutive
10-ms samples.
A forecast is declared stable if it avoids this trigger and the final 100
samples satisfy angle span below \(\pi\) and maximum absolute COI speed below
\(0.005\)~p.u. A numerically qualified forecast outside both conditions is
unsettled. Numerical failures are counted separately. With instability as
the positive class, confirmed recall is \(\mathrm{TP}/n_{\rm unstable}\)
and stable false-alarm rate is \(\mathrm{FP}/n_{\rm stable}\). Full-cohort
accuracy requires a valid decision on every event.

\section{Energy-Structured Grey-Box Model and Operator Library}
\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Shared electromechanical backbone}
Let \(B=[b_1,\ldots,b_{45}]\) be the oriented incidence matrix of the
complete generator graph, with \(d_e=b_e^\top q\) and
\(r_e=b_e^\top\delta^\star\). Edge \(e\) contributes a shifted potential
\begin{align}
 \Phi_j(d;r)&=\frac{\cos(jr)-\cos(j(r+d))}{j}
                  -d\sin(jr),\\
 U_0(q)&=\sum_{e=1}^{45} k_e^0\Phi_1(d_e;r_e),
 \qquad k_e^0>0.
 \label{eq:basic_potential}
\end{align}
The shift makes the edge force vanish at \(q=0\). For an orthonormal basis
\(N\) of the Euclidean zero-sum subspace of \(\mathbb R^{10}\), the basic
damping is \(D_0=N\diag(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_9)N^\top\), with
\(\beta_j\ge 0\). Ten reciprocal memory ports pair
\(C=P\diag(g_1,\ldots,g_{10})\) with the positive decay matrix
\(\Lambda=\diag(1/\tau_1,\ldots,1/\tau_{10})\).
Eliminating \(z\) from \(\dot z=C^\top v-\Lambda z\) gives the memory force
\begin{equation}
 Cz(t)=Ce^{-\Lambda t}z(0)+
    \int_0^t Ce^{-\Lambda(t-s)}C^\top v(s)\,ds.
 \label{eq:memory_kernel}
\end{equation}
The first term propagates the unresolved history at the initial time and the
second accumulates the subsequent rotor motion. The backbone has 74 shared
coefficients, comprising 45 edge stiffnesses, nine damping coefficients, ten
port gains, and ten time constants.

\subsection{Augmented dynamics and candidate operators}
For a support \(\mathcal S\subseteq\mathfrak G=\{\op{1},\ldots,\op{5}\}\),
let \(\chi=(c,\xi)\) collect the active common and spatial coupling states.
With all operators active, \(c\in\mathbb R\) and \(\xi\in\mathbb R^9\).
The augmented storage and equations are
\begin{align}
 H(q,v,z,\chi)&=\tfrac12 v^\top M v+U(q,\chi)+\tfrac12 z^\top z\nonumber\\
      &\quad+\tfrac12\kappa_c c^2+\tfrac12\kappa_\xi\|\xi\|_2^2,
      \label{eq:storage}\\
 \dot q&=v,\nonumber\\
 M\dot v&=P\bigl[p-\nabla_q U-D(q)v\bigr]-C(q)z,\nonumber\\
 \dot z&=C(q)^\top v-\Lambda z,\nonumber\\
 \dot\chi&=-G_\chi\nabla_\chi H. \label{eq:mother}
\end{align}
Here \(G_\chi=\diag(\mu_c,\mu_\xi I_9)\) on the active blocks.
Absent coupling blocks and their storage terms are dropped. The full system
has 40 coordinates and 38 independent states after the two COI constraints.
The variables \(z\) and \(\chi\) represent effective memory and coupling for
the observed motion. Their identification targets the input--output dynamics
of the retained machines.

The operator definitions in Table~\ref{tab:operators} share the same
mechanical and storage structure. For machine \(i\), define the local angular
excursion \(s_i(q)=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_i}d_e^2/|\mathcal E_i|\), where
\(\mathcal E_i\) is its set of incident edges.
\begin{itemize}
 \item \(\op{1}\) replaces \(\Phi_1\) by
       \((1-2a\cos(2r))\Phi_1+a\cos(r)\Phi_2\) on each edge.
       This adds a conservative second harmonic while preserving the
       equilibrium force and the linearized edge stiffness, with
       \(a\in[0,0.6]\).
 \item \(\op{2}\) makes damping decrease with the local angular excursion,
       \begin{equation}
        D(q)=D_0^{1/2}
           \left[I-\rho\diag\!\left(\frac{s_i(q)}{1+s_i(q)}\right)\right]
           D_0^{1/2}.
        \label{eq:damping}
       \end{equation}
       The bound \(0\le \rho\le0.85\) ensures
       \(D(q)\succeq(1-\rho)D_0\succeq0\).
 \item \(\op{3}\) contributes a common edge log-multiplier \(c\), and
       \(\op{4}\) contributes the zero-sum spatial pattern
       \(|B|^\top N_\chi\xi\), with \(N_\chi\) the Helmert basis of the
       zero-sum subspace and \(|B|\) the elementwise absolute incidence.
       Writing the combined pattern as \(u\), use
       \(k_e=k_e^0\exp(fu_e)\), where
       \begin{equation}
        f=\left[1+\sum_e\left\{
          \left(\frac{(u_e)_+}{\log 2}\right)^8+
          \left(\frac{(-u_e)_+}{\log 4}\right)^8
        \right\}\right]^{-1/8}.
        \label{eq:log_limit}
       \end{equation}
       The common scaling keeps each \(k_e/k_e^0\) within \([1/4,2]\).
       Both blocks move down the same storage gradient, with
       \(\mu_c,\mu_\xi\in[0,0.5]\), \(\kappa_c=1\), and
       \(\kappa_\xi\in[0.05,1]\).
 \item \(\op{5}\) makes the memory port state dependent through
       \(C(q)=P\diag(g_i/(1+\gamma_s s_i(q)))\), \(\gamma_s\in[0,8]\).
       The same attenuation appears in both directions of the port.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}
Every library support preserves the COI constraints and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \dot H={}&v^\top p-v^\top D(q)v-z^\top\Lambda z\\
           &-(\nabla_\chi H)^\top G_\chi\nabla_\chi H
           \le v^\top p.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:supply}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
The forces in \eqref{eq:mother} are zero-sum because
\(\mathbf{1}^\top P=0\) and \(\mathbf{1}^\top C(q)=0\). Thus
\(m^\top\dot v=0\), and \(\dot q=v\) preserves the angular constraint.
Differentiation of \eqref{eq:storage} gives the mechanical terms
\(v^\top M\dot v+v^\top\nabla_q U\), the memory term \(z^\top\dot z\), and
the coupling term \((\nabla_\chi H)^\top\dot\chi\).
Using \(v^\top P=v^\top\) cancels the potential forces. The two port terms
\(-v^\top C(q)z\) and \(z^\top C(q)^\top v\) cancel pointwise.
The remaining terms yield \eqref{eq:supply} by positive semidefiniteness.
\end{IEEEproof}

All 32 operator supports pass the synthetic storage-balance check
at tolerance \(10^{-12}\). For a constant input the tilted storage
\(H-p^\top q\) is nonincreasing. The linear reference shift in \(\Phi_1\)
permits an unbounded potential on the unwrapped angle space, so a global
stability inference requires separate lower-bound and invariance conditions.

\section{Causal Initialization and Automated Operator Discovery}
\label{sec:discovery}
\subsection{Nested estimation from a short history}
The support \(\mathcal S\) and shared coefficients \(\theta\) specify
both the history model and the forecast. Each evaluation of \((\theta,\mathcal
S)\) solves for the event's initial hidden state from its 100-ms observation
window. Model fitting minimizes a cross-event objective on the Training Set
through this causal solve. The Validation Set determines checkpoints and
support. In a diagnostic with freely fitted initial memory forces, small
port gains approaching \(10^{-5}\) could be compensated by large inferred
memory states. The median forecast-origin hidden storage in the Validation
Set reached \(4.19\times10^{11}\). Re-estimating memory from the short
history inside each shared-parameter update ties the training trajectory to
the initializer available at prediction.

Let \(y_j=(q_j,v_j)\) for \(j=0,\ldots,10\). Initialize the hidden coupling
states at zero and parameterize the initial memory as
\begin{equation}
 z_h(\eta)=\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{1+\|\eta\|_2^2/(2R)^2}}.
 \label{eq:soft_radius}
\end{equation}
Starting the ODE at \((y_0,z_h(\eta),0)\) defines its history prediction
\(\tilde y_j(\eta;\theta,\mathcal S)\). The inner objective is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1}{200}\sum_{j=1}^{10}
 \left\|W_h\bigl[\tilde y_j(\eta)-y_j\bigr]\right\|_2^2
       +\lambda_h\|\eta/R\|_2^2,\qquad
       \lambda_h=10^{-6},
 \label{eq:history}
\end{equation}
where \(W_h\) contains the inverse history scales fixed on the Training Set.
Let \(r_h\) be the residual vector stacking \(W_h(\tilde y_j-y_j)/\sqrt{200}\)
and \(\sqrt{\lambda_h}\eta/R\), and let \(J_h=\partial r_h/\partial\eta\).
Five Gauss--Newton iterations from \(\eta=0\) use
\(\Delta\eta=\arg\min_d\|r_h+J_hd\|_2^2\). The analytic ODE tangent and
the derivative of \eqref{eq:soft_radius} supply \(J_h\). The fitted history
is reintegrated through \(t_o\), and its terminal hidden state is paired with
the measured \((q_{10},v_{10})\).

The hard-radius solver projects the history-start memory onto the ball
\(\|z_h\|_2\le R\). In a matched initializer diagnostic, this saturated
at \(R^2/2=21.85\) in the model's storage units on a missed unstable
event. The smooth map raises the admissible memory norm toward \(2R\) and
keeps the dependence on the estimated coordinates differentiable.
The fixed light prior damps weakly observed directions in the unconstrained
coordinates \(\eta/R\). This freedom changes the hidden force that is
carried to the forecast origin, where the visible kinetic energy is set by
the observed speed.

\subsection{First-swing loss and local backbone fitting}
The Training Set contains 311 stable and 35 unstable events. A gradient
diagnostic on this set at a shared starting equation found norms of 15.81,
0.24, and 1.33 for weighted trajectory squared error, one-sided speed error,
and weak momentum, respectively. Their partial derivatives with respect to
the log damping multiplier were \(-4.95\), \(+0.19\), and \(+1.05\).
At this point, descent on the trajectory term favors larger damping, while
the first-swing terms favor smaller damping. This local conflict motivates
balancing their influence at the beginning of a fit and selecting the
checkpoint using free trajectories.

The trajectory loss \(\mathcal L_{\rm tr}\) averages the 1- and 3-s
event losses on the available samples. It uses a unit-transition Huber
penalty on normalized residuals for stable events and squared error for
unstable events. The weak-form term evaluates the residual
\begin{equation}
 r_{ab}=v(t_b)-v(t_a)
       -\int_{t_a}^{t_b}M^{-1}F(q,v,h;p)\,dt,
 \label{eq:weak_momentum}
\end{equation}
where \(F\) is the mechanical force in \eqref{eq:mother} and \(h=(z,\chi)\)
evolves along linearly interpolated measured \((q,v)\), starting with
the causal forecast-origin hidden state. Dividing momentum by machine mass
gives a residual in speed units. The loss \(\mathcal L_{\rm mom}\) averages
its normalized squared components over the complete 0--200 and
200--400~ms windows after \(t_o\). On the free rollout over the first
500~ms, \(\mathcal L_{\rm vel}\) penalizes positive
\(\operatorname{sgn}(v_i)(v_i-\hat v_i)\) after speed normalization.
Both losses weight an unstable event eight times as much as a stable one.
With \(\mathcal L_{\rm fs}=\mathcal L_{\rm mom}+\tfrac12\mathcal L_{\rm vel}\),
the outer objective is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L&=\mathcal L_{\rm tr}+\alpha\mathcal L_{\rm fs}
                 +\|\theta-\theta_{\rm a}\|_2^2+0.1\mathcal L_E,\\
 \alpha&=1.05\left.
          \frac{\|\nabla\mathcal L_{\rm tr}\|_2}{\|\nabla\mathcal L_{\rm fs}\|_2}
          \right|_{\rm start}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
The anchor \(\theta_{\rm a}\) is expressed in the fit coordinates, with
independent log stiffness and damping factors centered at a reference
backbone. The stiffness and damping multipliers stay within \([0.5,2]\)
relative to their anchors, \(g_i\ge0.01\), and
\(\tau_i\in[0.02,5]\)~s. The energy guard \(\mathcal L_E\) uses a squared
positive excess of the origin memory energy relative to
\(\tfrac12(2R)^2\). The gradient calibration uses the full Training Set
at the start of each fit and is subsequently held fixed.

\subsection{Candidate screening, curriculum, and pruning}
Fig.~\ref{fig:method} and Algorithm~\ref{alg:discovery} summarize the
offline selection and the causal forecast. The weak-momentum screen fixes
the backbone and evaluates each operator individually against the model
with all five switches off. A coefficient grid and local midpoint refinements
are ranked by the weighted integral residual. The final support candidates
use blocks whose best point improves unstable-event RMS at both numerical
step sizes. For backbone fitting, \(\op{2}\) supplies the damping law with
\(\rho=2/3\). The first stage opens the 45 stiffness and nine damping
coefficients to let different edges adapt to different disturbance
patterns. The second stage also opens the gains and decay times.
Each stage has 12 full Training Set updates, with a complete Validation
Set check every three updates.

The final backward comparison starts the screened support and the nested
deletions from the same local backbone. Each candidate receives six full
updates and a checkpoint check at steps 0, 3, and 6. Eligibility at both
step sizes requires numerical qualification, \(12/12\) Validation Set
instabilities confirmed, at most \(2/95\) stable false alarms, zero unsettled
Validation Set cases, and at least \(33/35\) Training Set instabilities
confirmed. The Validation Set 8-s stable median and true-negative mean
NRMSE must be at most 0.21 and 0.24, respectively. The signed median speed
residual across 33 preselected Training Set separation windows must move
at least 20\% toward zero, with the median absolute residual also improving.
For eligible supports with equal false-alarm counts, a deletion is
preferred when the stable median, true-negative mean, and first-500-ms
peak deficit each stay within 2\% of the larger support. These criteria
are applied before the frozen Test Set evaluation.

\subsection{Numerical qualification}
We use fourth-order Runge--Kutta in CUDA double precision, with a
5-ms training step. Formal ODE forecasts use a 2.5-ms main step and
a 1.25-ms check, sampled onto the common 10-ms output grid. The
inter-step COI NRMSE and normalized common-speed discrepancy must both
be at most \(10^{-3}\). Classification must agree and paired triggers
must lie within 20~ms. For classification after a trigger, the discrepancy
is checked through the later trigger. For forecasts without a trigger it is
checked through the end. Trajectory metrics have their own interval-level
qualification and full-forecast finiteness is recorded separately.

\section{Synthetic Validation and Identifiability Boundaries}
\label{sec:synthetic}
The ten-machine synthetic study fits the eight supports of
\(\{\op{3},\op{4},\op{5}\}\) to five known-truth systems (S0--S4).
Each truth has 24 fitting, 12 selection, and 12 held-out events.
Selection uses the 3-s trajectory squared error. In S2 the selected
model retains the saturating memory port, learning \(\gamma_s=0.464\)
against a truth of 0.60. The resulting memory kernels along the fitting
and selection paths differ from the truth by 1.55\%. The five selected
equations each classify all 12 held-out events correctly. The largest
normalized storage residual across these experiments is
\(4.29\times10^{-16}\).
With known coefficients and a history solve that also estimates
the coupling coordinates, a separate experiment selects the four
fixed, common, spatial, and mixed coupling patterns. The mean
selection-split 8-s errors range from \(4.40\times10^{-9}\) to
\(6.56\times10^{-6}\) (Table~\ref{tab:synthetic}). The second harmonic
and state-modulated damping are also selected in their respective
known-truth systems in a separate two-operator study.
\SyntheticTable

The fifth-harmonic truth in S4 tests whether the candidate library is
adequate. At least one in-library candidate passes the long-horizon
trajectory and timing criteria on these events. In the known-coefficient
coupling study, all four in-library candidates exceed \(10^{-4}\) on the
maximum normalized 200-ms momentum error across the selection events
and both step sizes. The fixed-coupling candidate reaches
\(8.79\times10^{-4}\). The integral balance over the short window reveals a
missing restoring-force term under excitation where the 8-s accuracy
criterion accepts an approximation.

These experiments distinguish three forms of recovery. With common and
spatial coupling both active, the known-coefficient study obtains a
selection-split 8-s error of \(6.56\times10^{-6}\) while the worst
spatial hidden-state error reaches 38.2\%. In the unknown-coefficient fits
on S1 and S3, selecting by 3-s trajectory error drops the true spatial
block, which indicates that the excitation and target metric provide weak
evidence for its coefficients. Finally, the prior matters most along
low-sensitivity directions. In a fixed-coefficient S3 comparison,
removing the prior gives a clean-history 8-s error of
\(1.11\times10^{-8}\), whereas the same change under history noise with
standard deviation \(10^{-3}\) of the training history scales raises the
error from 0.001714 to 0.005741. The resulting accuracy of the observed
trajectory and the recovery of the hidden coordinates test different
properties of the fit.

\section{Case Study on the IEEE 39-Bus System}
\label{sec:ieee39}
\subsection{Dataset construction and benchmark setup}
We use a higher-order IEEE 39-bus, ten-generator model in ANDES 2.0.0
\cite{cui2021andes}. The Training Set has 346 events (311 stable and
35 unstable). The Validation Set has 107 events (95 stable and
12 unstable) from eight fault groups and is used for model and threshold
selection. The Test Set is generated separately at the same operating
point, with three-phase bus faults and restored post-clearing topology.
Its 94 events include 62 stable and 32 unstable events in 21 bus--line fault
groups, with seven buses absent from the fitting and selection sets and 26
near-critical fault conditions. Of the 32 unstable events, 31 trigger
within 0.2--1.5~s of clearing. The generation protocol fixes the fault
conditions and inclusion quotas in advance, checks disjointness from the
fitting and selection events, and retains labels from the higher-order
simulation with a 15-s window for confirming stable truth.
The models and operating thresholds are frozen before Test Set inference.
The benchmark uses the same observation horizon, origin mechanical power,
COI scoring, label rule for trajectories, and Test Set for all applicable
models. The class proportions reflect the designed fault sample.

\subsection{Operator selection and component ablations}
The \(\op{2}\) block gives the largest improvement in the
Training Set weak-momentum screen, reducing unstable-event RMS by 37.54\%
and stable-event RMS by 7.33\%. The saturation and common-coupling operators
\(\op{5}\) and \(\op{3}\) also improve unstable-event RMS, while
\(\op{1}\) and \(\op{4}\) increase it (Table~\ref{tab:operators}).
The 54-coefficient curriculum reduces the median signed separation-speed
residual across 33 Training Set windows from \(+2.551\) to
\(+1.544\)~rad/s relative to the hard-radius reference. The largest
individual stiffness adjustment from the backbone anchor is 1.22\%.
The selected checkpoints are update 12 of the local fit and update 0 of
the 74-coefficient fit (Fig.~\ref{fig:curriculum}), preserving the
starting memory gains and time constants. Neither of the larger screened
supports confirms all 12 Validation Set instabilities. The full screened
support has true-negative mean 8-s NRMSE 0.31277, compared with 0.21494
for the single \(\op{2}\) support. The latter meets the stated constraints,
confirms \(33/35\) Training Set instabilities and \(12/12\) Validation
Set instabilities, and gives a Validation Set stable median of 0.19026
with \(2/95\) false alarms. Its selected pruning checkpoint carries forward
the local backbone at update 0.

\label{sec:physics}
\subsection{Dominant electromechanical modes}
We linearize the frozen equations at the reference equilibrium and
zero projected disturbance input. The Jacobian has dimension 28 after
eliminating the two COI constraints, and its analytic and automatic
derivatives differ by less than \(1.5\times10^{-14}\). Experimental
mode estimates use a block-Hankel shift identification of the
reference simulated rotor speeds \cite{juang1985era}, using
0.5--8~s and 1.5--8~s post-origin windows and ranks 6, 10, and 14.
We assign modes by frequency, damping, and the modal assurance
criterion (MAC), which is the squared normalized inner product of two
complex mode shapes. Strong matches use non-growing model modes with
relative frequency error at most 10\%, absolute damping-ratio error
at most 0.05, and \(\mathrm{MAC}\ge0.80\).
The Test Set yields usable reference modes for 53 of the 62 stable
events. On 52 commonly matched dominant modes, ES-ROM lowers median
absolute frequency error from the \hard{}'s 0.0506 to 0.0318~Hz
(7.62\% to 4.79\% relative error), with median MAC 0.9452. Strong
matches rise from \(41/62\) to \(52/62\).
For the 63 common matches in the Validation Set, the respective median
errors are 0.05023 and 0.03292~Hz, with 62 of 63 improving.
The softening term vanishes at \(q=0\), linking the spectral changes
to backbone calibration (Table~\ref{tab:physics}).

\subsection{Effective coupling and inertia-weighted coherency}
Among the ten largest absolute edge adjustments from the stiffness
anchor, five edges incident on the large-inertia external equivalent
generator (G10) soften by 1.17--1.22\%. The other five, linking
generators within New England, stiffen by 1.17--1.22\%
(Fig.~\ref{fig:stiffness}). The fitted changes thus distinguish the
inter-area connection pattern within the effective graph.
We compare bipartitions induced by the second eigenvectors of the
ordinary stiffness Laplacian and the generalized problem
\(L(k^0)u=\lambda M u\) \cite{chow1982coherency}. The observed
reference groups use the final 2~s of COI speed in the 95 stable
Validation Set events. Including inertia improves agreement from
\(21/45\) to \(29/45\) generator pairs and increases the adjusted
Rand index from \(-0.1111\) to \(0.2593\) (Fig.~\ref{fig:coherency}).
The group containing G10 contracts from seven machines to
\(\{\mathrm{G1},\mathrm{G2},\mathrm{G10}\}\), while the observed tail
groups G10 separately. The ratio \(\lambda_3/\lambda_2\approx1.15\)
indicates closely spaced modes of the stiffness--inertia problem.
The full linearization also includes damping and memory.

\subsection{Forecast-origin mechanical energy and memory storage}
At \(t_o\), \(E_{\rm mech}=\tfrac12 v^\top Mv+U(q)\) evaluates the
measured motion in the frozen backbone. The remaining part of \(H\)
is the inferred memory energy \(E_z=\tfrac12\|z(t_o)\|_2^2\).
The hard and soft ablations use identical mechanical energies event by
event, as they share the backbone and visible origin. Their Test Set
mechanical-energy AUROC is 0.9526, the same as ES-ROM to the shown
precision. Changing the initializer alone moves total-storage AUROC
from 0.8926 to 0.7157. For ES-ROM the total and memory energies reach
0.6956 and 0.5766. Fig.~\ref{fig:energy} shows how a wider admissible
memory state changes the energy distributions while the mechanical term
continues to rank the unstable cases highly. The paired initializer
contrast supports interpreting \(E_z\) as storage associated with the
effective memory force. The mechanical-energy AUROC measures ranking
on this fault distribution.

\subsection{Methodological scope}
The fitted law covers post-clearing motion at the restored topology.
Critical clearing time requires fault-on dynamics and a switching rule.
The 100-ms constant-power protocol classifies three Test Set
instabilities as stable. During deep terminal-voltage depression and
large swings, the shifted-sine potential assumes fixed equivalent
internal voltage and can overstate restoring torque. It omits
nonconservative transfer conductance. Constant mechanical power from
\(t_o=0.10\)~s omits later governor action, and the model omits late
excitation limits during borderline swings. Reciprocal ports and
positive-semidefinite damping ensure \(\dot{H}\le v^\top p\) across
supports, covering damped recovery and first-swing separation.
Control-driven negative damping of low-frequency modes requires active
energy input. The Test Set covers unseen faults at one ten-generator
operating point. Other operating points, larger grids, and inverter-rich
networks require separate validation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
ES-ROM selects a shared explicit post-fault equation with 74 backbone
coefficients and one local damping softening coefficient. With 100~ms
of observations, it confirms 90.62\% of Test Set instabilities at
zero observed stable false alarms and 96.81\% accuracy. The same
frozen equation gives the mode, cut, and energy information needed
to interpret the forecast.

A switched fault-on and post-fault energy model would support
clearing-time studies. Extensions include dynamic mechanical-power
states for later governor action, voltage-sensitive or nonconservative
terms for severe voltage depression, and bounded non-passive ports with
closed-loop stability certificates for active oscillations. Sparse
graphs and inverter-control operators would test momentum screening
and energy-consistent selection on larger, heterogeneous grids.
