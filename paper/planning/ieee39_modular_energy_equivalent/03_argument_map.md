# Argument map

## Scientific tension

- What is known: physically structured reduced models can reproduce selected IEEE39 transient regimes, and explicit energy structures can pass local numerical identities.
- What is unknown: whether one data-derived, modular equation can cover the combined observed transient corpus while retaining identifiable modes and a useful integration-free stability certificate.
- Why the gap matters: a model that is accurate only under a narrow input treatment, or a certificate that declares almost nothing stable, does not provide a deployable engineering equivalent.

## Central research question

Can one ten-generator, module-union, energy-structured model learned without test leakage reproduce the IEEE39 post-clear transient corpus and provide non-vacuous integration-free stability margins inside a certified excitation envelope?

## Central thesis

The planned thesis is that excitation-local evidence can be composed as removable physical blocks in one shared equation, and that joint closed-loop, identifiability and energy gates can retain only the blocks required to reproduce the corpus and certify stability. This remains a hypothesis until P4–P6 pass.

## Supporting arguments

### Argument 1: spatial fidelity precedes parameter reduction
- Claim: the current corpus requires ten-generator state resolution before modular parameter pruning.
- Evidence: the focused cluster-loss validation selected the identity partition and was stable across registered role seeds and sensitivities.
- Limitation: this conclusion is corpus-specific and does not forbid clustering on another supplied corpus.

### Argument 2: mechanism evidence should be composed in one equation
- Claim: different transients can expose different storage, dissipation and memory mechanisms without implying different underlying systems.
- Evidence: prior AFSD/ESC analyses show excitation-dependent observability and failure of a universal per-trajectory DMD mode; owner constraint fixes one model.
- Limitation: the union may be over-parameterised unless block support, counterfactual effect and deletion are audited.

### Argument 3: trajectory and certificate must pass jointly
- Claim: energy identities alone are insufficient, and accuracy alone does not justify integration-free stability claims.
- Evidence: M2A-LYAP and the current combined run separately demonstrate this tension.
- Limitation: the required Level 6 IEEE39 audit dataset does not yet exist.

### Argument 4: leakage-free five-fold evidence is necessary for the paper claim
- Claim: F1 development results cannot support the final engineering conclusion.
- Evidence: existing accepted anchors and current failures are validation-only; project contract locks test and other folds during selection.
- Limitation: the final confirmation is fixed to seed 324, so the paper will not claim broad random-seed robustness.

## Counterarguments / alternative explanations

- A simple ten-machine baseline may be sufficient. The method therefore includes a `B2_SUFFICIENT` honest terminal state and requires nontrivial module contribution before promotion.
- Long-horizon error may be caused mainly by mechanical-power closure rather than missing electrical states. P1/P2 isolate the causal input policy before expanding the state model.
- A conservative certificate can achieve zero missed instability by certifying nothing. Level 6 therefore includes stable-region coverage and margin-accuracy gates.
- Apparent mode mismatch may reflect excitation non-observability rather than wrong equation poles. Modal evaluation separates shared poles from excitation-local empirical visibility.

## Final move

If P4–P6 pass, the reader should conclude that the registered modular union and Level 6 procedure yield one leakage-audited IEEE39 engineering equivalent inside a stated envelope. If any central gate fails, the paper conclusion is narrowed to the supported negative result and the manuscript does not claim a completed stability-certified equivalent.
